## Core Source Ideas

- Easy runs can serve two different purposes:
  - recovery: move gently, increase blood flow, feel better for the next hard session;
  - development: build long-term aerobic capacity, cardiovascular support, mitochondria, capillaries, and oxygen delivery.
- Training zones are rough categories, not strict on/off switches.
- Long-term aerobic development usually comes from accumulating a lot of relatively easy training over time.
- Intensity can build aerobic fitness quickly, but the gains often level off; easy aerobic training improves more gradually and is safer over the long term.
- For many amateur runners, the weak link is not speed, but aerobic foundation, mechanical tolerance, recovery ability, time, and life stress.
- Easy-run duration depends on purpose. A useful stimulus often needs roughly 25-30 minutes or more; many easy runs can sit around 45-60 minutes, with occasional longer runs.
- If easy runs make fatigue accumulate during a mostly easy phase, they are probably too fast.
- Once an athlete has already built a large aerobic base, some easy runs become more about recovery and maintenance; pace then matters even less.
