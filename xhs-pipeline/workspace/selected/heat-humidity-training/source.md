## Supporting Fact Checks

- CDC / NIOSH heat acclimatization page:
  - URL: https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/recommendations/acclimatization.html
  - Useful facts:
    - Heat acclimatization means beneficial physiological adaptations after repeated hot-environment exposure.
    - Adaptations include earlier and more efficient sweating, reduced electrolyte loss in sweat, stabilized circulation, ability to work at lower core temperature and heart rate, and increased skin blood flow.
    - Gradual exposure over 7-14 days is recommended as a general acclimatization window.

- CDC Travelers' Health heat illness page:
  - URL: https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/heat-illnesses
  - Useful facts:
    - Heat illness risk depends on environment, activity, hydration, and individual condition.
    - Warning symptoms such as dizziness, nausea, confusion, and heat-stroke-like signs should be treated seriously.

- Strength Running: Everything You Need to Know About Running in the Heat This Summer
  - URL: https://strengthrunning.com/2014/06/running-in-the-heat/
  - Useful facts:
    - Humidity reduces sweat evaporation, making cooling less effective.
    - Heat, sun exposure, dehydration, and cardiac drift can make the same run feel much harder.
